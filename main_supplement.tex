\section*{S2. Calibration Anchors vs.\ Held-Out Benchmark Validation (24 Checks)}
All 24 quantitative checks generated by \texttt{experiments/validate\_simulator.py} (saved in \texttt{validation\_benchmarks.csv}) appear in Table~\ref{tab:supp_val}, explicitly separated into \textbf{19 calibration anchors} (fitted directly from Splitwise Table~IV, Figs.~8--9 power-cap curves, and ServerlessLLM Fig.~6a checkpoint loader timings) and \textbf{5 held-out benchmark sweeps} (comparing simulated \texttt{B2} vs.\ \texttt{B1} energy-per-token across $0.25\times$--$2.00\times$ RPS against Li et al.'s empirical trends, which were not used during parameter fitting). Across uncontended single-request Splitwise Table~IV points, simulated TTFT includes parameterized KV stall overhead (+5.0~ms on H100, +8.0~ms on A100), yielding a $+8.81\%$ to $+14.24\%$ positive offset above raw Splitwise compute latencies, while nominal $28/42$~ms decode step calibration differs from code-workload reference TPOT ($-9.68\%$ H100, $-19.23\%$ A100). Under power capping, a $0.55\times$ TDP decode cap leaves 16-request decode step latency flat at 29.25~ms while dropping node power from 1563.5~W to 1069.8~W ($-31.6\%$), before an abrupt $+6.0\%$ step penalty appears at $0.40\times$ TDP (31.01~ms), matching Splitwise Figs.~8--9.
